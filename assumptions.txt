79. The simulated vendor is rule-based: a hidden reserve and a fixed flexibility per bid (hash of the bid id; the two hero best-bidders are pinned so the demo story is 270 for the buy and 168 for the sell). It accepts any offer that meets its reserve, may grant up to 15 days better payment, and calls its price final within 0.3% of its reserve or after five replies.
    Reason: a deterministic vendor makes the demo repeatable and the tests exact, and the reserve never leaves the backend.

80. Buyer tactics: open at the target; move 40% of the way toward the vendor each round (25% when the objective is payment terms); when the vendor is within 2.5% of the limit, ask exactly the limit with one payment tier better; accept an in-limit price after round 3, when the vendor says final, or when it meets the target; otherwise hand back.
    Reason: simple, explainable rules the buyer can follow and predict.

81. Accepting the best quote as it stands is allowed from `analyzed` and produces a non-negotiated outcome (original equals final, duration 0). It is the buyer's own explicit decision and is not limit-checked: the limit governs only what the assistant offers or accepts inside a conversation.
    Reason: the buyer may always take a quote they can see; the limit is a guardrail for the assistant, not for the buyer.

82. Sessions, turns and drafts live only in the store and are cleared by Reset. Seeded items that are already `negotiating` can be started; seeded `handed_back` items need the buyer to re-set the points first (the state becomes `analyzed`) before a new session can start, or they can be closed without a deal.
    Reason: the seed data has no conversation history, and the POC keeps no data beyond the store.

83. The best quote is the lowest raw unit price when buying and the highest when selling (what the item view shows as the best bid). A negotiation started without naming a vendor uses the vendor of that quote (ties go to the lower bid id), and that quote is the session's `original_price`, which is the baseline savings or uplift are measured from.
    Reason: one definition used by the item view, the default vendor, accept-as-is and the savings figures, so the numbers on screen agree.

84. Continuing from an agreed result sets `continuing` on the session. The next move asks for a price strictly better than the agreed one (40% of the way toward the target, never past it); if the agreed price already meets the target, or the move would round to the same price, it accepts instead. The flag clears when an offer is sent.
    Reason: "keep negotiating" must try to get more, not repeat the acceptance at the old price.

85. Closing an item without a deal (only from `handed_back`) records no outcome. The item counts as nothing in the event's quoted, original and final values, adds no savings, and is not counted as negotiated.
    Reason: nothing was bought or sold, so it must not inflate or distort any total.

86. Every offer must be strictly better for us than the vendor's current price; otherwise the buyer is told to accept that price instead. When the vendor's quote already meets the target, the assistant's first move is to propose accepting it rather than an opening offer.
    Reason: asking for the price the vendor already gave, or a worse one, would be a step backwards.

87. Anything else is refused with the number named.
    Reason: the text must never say a different price from the structured offer the vendor is answering.

97. 98. A "Next step" bar under the progress steps says what to do now. A new Negotiations page lists every conversation across events, and the event page lists the ones on that event.
    Reason: buyers could not find vendors, items or conversation history; each now has one obvious place.

99. Negotiations are paced like real ones. The vendor first answers an opening offer that is more than 3% away from its quote with a pushback and no movement. After that it gives up only part of its flexibility per round, and even when our offer already meets its hidden reserve it counters once or twice more before agreeing. We concede 20% of the distance per round, give a different reason in each message (market rates, volume, relationship), and only ask for the final close after the third round. Hero results: BUY agrees at 270 with ZD45 after five rounds (savings 9,000; continue lands at 269), SELL agrees at 167 after four rounds (uplift 20,000; continue lands at 168). This replaces the earlier short 2-round hero paths (270 and 168).
    Reason: the buyer expects a believable back-and-forth, not a two-message exchange; all prices still stay inside the buyer's limit and the vendor's reserve is never revealed.

100. Everyday questions are handled from the quote on file. The vendor asks about quantity and delivery once (after its first pushback) and our next message answers from the item; we ask about delivery in round 2 and about warranty (buy) or bid validity (sell) in round 4, and the vendor answers from its quote. The buyer can also ask any question from the workspace (quick chips or free text): topics are recognised by keywords in English, Hindi and Marathi (delivery or pickup, payment, quantity, warranty, validity, incoterm) and answered from the bid; an unrecognised question gets a polite "let me check and get back". Questions never change the price or the round, and they go through the same guardrails as offers.
    Reason: real negotiations mix price talk with practical questions; nothing here is invented, and no internal number can be asked about or revealed.

101. The conversation language can be switched in the workspace at any time (English, Hindi, Marathi). It applies to the messages that follow, and a waiting draft is rewritten; what was already said is not translated. Text the buyer types is sent as typed. Only the standard wording is translated; the extra reasons and varied phrasings exist in English only.
    Reason: translation of free text is out of scope for the POC.

102. Messages are chat messages, not emails: no "Regards", no sign-off and no signature block (this replaces assumption 88; the NEGOTIATION_SIGNATURE setting is gone). The opening still greets the vendor by name. In the workspace the buyer sees a chat-style message bar: a "+" menu (add a price offer, suggested messages), the text field and a send button that becomes a stop button while the conversation runs on its own. Pressing stop switches to manual and enables typing. Typed text with no price is sent as a question and answered from the quote; a request to do better on price is answered by asking for a number.
    Reason: the buyer asked for a messaging feel and a visible way for a person watching to take over at any moment.

103. The approval page is a review screen: a table of the items waiting for approval (counterparty, quantity, original and final price, savings or uplift, value, terms) and, for the selected row, tabs for Summary (counterparty details, agreed terms, negotiation record, event details), Conversation, Comparison and Price history. Values shown (value at the final price) come from the backend; the browser only displays them.
    Reason: the buyer asked for everything needed to approve a deal in one place.

104. Charts use one bright categorical palette (tokens --c1 to --c6) and are interactive: the category donut lifts a slice on hover and shows its share at the centre; the price-history chart is a smooth area chart with a crosshair and tooltip, range buttons (3M, 6M, 1Y, All), keyboard arrows and reference lines for target and limit. The donut shows the five largest categories; the rest stays as the empty part of the ring.
    Reason: readability and a modern feel; no extra totals are computed in the browser.

105. List screens share one pattern: tabs for All, Purchase events and Scrap events; a search box in the table header with a separate filter button (a menu of filters, each showing its current choice); sortable column headers (click once for ascending, again for descending, a third time to clear; empty values always stay last); and icon buttons for row actions. The dashboard shows the events table full width and the summary widgets below it; page titles carry no tagline and panels carry no subtitle. Date filtering uses a custom calendar with presets (all dates, last 30 days, last 90 days, this month, this year).
    Reason: a cleaner, denser layout that uses the full screen width; wording now says "Create" instead of "Simulate".

106. The filter menu opens its options in a side panel on hover (and on focus or click), and drills in on narrow screens. Summary tiles have a large icon on the left, the label, the value and a line of smaller figures, and each links to the data behind it (events, comparison, reports, negotiations, history). Corner radii use the names rounded-card and rounded-chip, because the earlier names clashed with Tailwind's own rounded-l and rounded-s (left-side and start-side corners), which is why some corners were square.
    Reason: fix for uneven corners reported on the dashboard, and the requested hover and drill-down behaviour.

107. Tables with many rows page their data in the browser: rows per page (10, 25, 50 or 100, remembered between visits), first, previous, numbered, next and last buttons with gaps shown as "…", a jump-to-page box once there are more than seven pages, and a "Showing 11–20 of 85" line. Sorting orders all rows before paging; a new sort, search, filter or page size returns to page one. It is on the events, vendors, history, comparison and negotiations tables; the dashboard events table is paged the same way.
    Reason: client-side paging is enough for the proof-of-concept data size; server-side paging would be the next step for real volumes.

108. Rows per page defaults to "Auto": the table shows as many rows as fit between its header and the bottom of the window (at least 5, at most 100), recalculated when the window is resized, and the footer shows the figure as "Auto (10)". A fixed size (10, 25, 50 or 100) can still be chosen; the choice is remembered. This refines assumption 107.
    Reason: the buyer asked for the number of records to follow the screen size.

109. A guided Demo story page walks through the whole process in nine steps (event, goals, vendor responses, compare and analyse, start, conversation, result, approval, closed and exported) for a purchase event (lunch buffet, target 250, ceiling 270) and a scrap event (aluminium lot, target 170, floor 165). Each step does the real action through the normal API; the current step is derived from the item's state. "Restart story" resets all demo data. The purchase story's last step also takes the best quote on the event's other items using their suggested goals, so the event can close and export.
    Reason: a repeatable client presentation that needs no setup or explanation from the presenter.

110. Events can be added by hand: Create event > Add purchase cart / Add scrap lot opens a four-step form (details, items, vendors, review). The buyer picks the category, plant and purchasing group (taken from existing events), requestor, cost centre, needed-by date and, for purchases, an optional cart number; adds items with quantity, unit and reference price (a scrap lot is one item in KG or TON); and chooses which vendors of that category to invite (at least three). The vendors' answers are still simulated: each invited vendor gets a scripted quote or bid built from the reference price, which is "created" later as for any other event. The earlier one-click sample events remain as "Create sample ..." entries. Eligibility (value band, enough quotes) is checked on the event page as before.
    Reason: a real intake step instead of an auto-generated event, without needing real SAP or vendor connections.

111. Hard vendors. Each simulated vendor has a hidden persona (cooperative, anchor-and-hold, bluffer, terms trader, deadline-driven, relationship seeker) chosen deterministically from its bid; vendors flagged as hard to move (V008, V037, V024, V003, V042) draw from the stubborn personas, and the first two demo bids stay cooperative. The persona only shapes how fast and in what way the vendor moves: its reserve is never crossed and is never shown. Our side follows phases (opening, probing, trading, pressing, closing) with tactics: terms trade (same price, better payment), "other offers" only when another vendor has really quoted (no number named), splitting the difference only when the midpoint is inside the limit, testing a "final price" called before round 5 once, restating the limit firmly, and handing back with ranked options (accept as is, switch to the next-best quote, trade terms, adjust the limit, close without a deal). Round cap 12; four replies in a row without movement ends the attempt. Switching vendor is only suggested. This replaces the earlier short-conversation limits (assumptions 99 and 108 stay valid for the first two demo stories).
    Reason: believable, longer negotiations with hard vendors while every price stays inside the buyer's limit.

112. History-based difficulty. For each vendor, the average share of the original price that past negotiated deals moved (deal.concession_share) gives a level: hard (under 2.5%), firm (under 4.5%), flexible, or unknown (fewer than 3 negotiated deals). The note ("Hard to crack: in 11 negotiated deals the price moved only 1.1% on average...") shows on the vendor lists, in the start panel and in the workspace strategy panel. The seeded history was adjusted so the five hard vendors really show small past concessions; no other seeded history changed.
    Reason: a recommendation grounded in what actually happened before, which also matches how the simulated vendor behaves.

113. The workspace has a Strategy panel (round of 12, phase, the vendor's observed stance, the history note, the next-best quote and the tactics used) and each of our messages carries a small tactic tag for the buyer only. Steering buttons (be firmer, offer terms) were left out; the manual message bar covers them.
    Reason: show the thinking without adding more controls.

114. The Add event form is grouped into sections with required markers, inline messages shown after the first attempt to continue, a calendar date picker with quick choices (in a week, two weeks, a month), suggested requestors, an items grid with one header row, searchable vendor cards, and a live "Your event" summary beside it. "Auto fill example" fills every step with a believable example for the chosen type (random category, plant, requestor, a date one to four weeks out, one to three items with quantities and prices near the examples, all vendors) and opens the review step; it creates nothing until "Create event" is pressed.
    Reason: faster demos and a clearer form.

115. Vendors for a new event are suggested automatically. Every vendor that deals in the chosen category is scored for the request: rating, how many deals it has done with us, past deals on the same or similar items (matched on the item descriptions typed in the form), past deals in the category, and how it has negotiated before (flexible adds, hard to move subtracts). The top five (never fewer than three) are preselected and marked Recommended with their reasons; the rest stay available, ranked below. The buyer can change the selection; once changed by hand, it is no longer overwritten. A vendor known to be hard to crack can still be recommended, with a "keep as a back-up" reason.
    Reason: the buyer should not have to know which vendors fit a request; the ranking uses only data already on record.

116. A category can be added from the Add event form: the last row of the Category list is "Add category", which turns into a text box with an enter button in the same place; the list shows every option without a scroll bar. A typed-in category has no history or examples, so any vendor of the right kind (suppliers for a purchase, scrap buyers for a scrap lot) can be invited and the vendors are ranked by rating and dealings only. It lives with the events created from it (category "Custom - Name" for a purchase, "Scrap - Name" for a lot); it is not kept as a reusable option for later forms.
    Reason: the fixed list cannot cover every request in a demo.

117. The Add event form starts with defaults: the requestor is the signed-in buyer, the needed-by date is two weeks out, a purchase cart gets the next free shopping cart number (editable; a number already used by another event is refused), and the event title is filled in and follows the first item until it is edited (a scrap lot starts with "Scrap - ", for example "Scrap - Aluminium Turnings Lot - 5,000 kg"; a purchase cart takes its first item's name). Auto fill resets the title and cart number to these defaults.
    Reason: fewer fields to type, and consistent naming and numbering.
